# Interview questions

Use these questions for the interviewer. Questions 1 to 6 per role come from the interview plan, then follow-up probes and the closing questions.

Rules for the interviewer:

- Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer.
- Ask "why?" and "can you give an example?". Ask for numbers, frequencies and names of reports.
- Do not suggest technical solutions and do not argue with the answer.
- Aim for 8 to 12 exchanges per role (about 30 to 40 minutes). Finish with the closing questions, then write END INTERVIEW.
- The note-taker writes notes during the interview using [the template](../_template.md).

## S1. Owner / CEO

**Core questions**

1. Which three decisions do you make each quarter that depend on sales or margin figures?
2. How do you judge today whether a channel or category is profitable?
3. Which costs (delivery, returns, advertising, marketplace commissions) should count in "real" profit?
4. What would a good monthly management report show? Which five numbers first?
5. Which comparisons matter most: vs last year, vs plan, own store vs marketplace, B2C vs B2B?
6. Who may see which data, and what are your concerns about personal data?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Which three numbers would you look at first thing on Monday?
2. Give an example of a decision you made last year with the wrong numbers.
3. What would "profitable channel" have to include to convince you?

## S2. E-commerce Manager

**Core questions**

1. Which steps of the customer journey do you monitor today?
2. How do you define a session and a conversion?
3. Which breakdowns do you need: device, traffic source, new vs returning?
4. How do you treat guest versus registered customers?
5. On which measures should the own store be compared with the marketplace?
6. What would you change in your work if you saw a funnel and abandoned-cart report every week?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Walk me through one weekly funnel review you would like to have.
2. What do you do today when conversion drops?
3. How do you tell a guest buyer from a returning customer today?

## S3. Category Manager (buyer)

**Core questions**

1. Which assortment decisions (add, delist, reprice, reorder) do you make every week?
2. Which numbers do you look at today and where do they come from?
3. How do you identify the best and worst products: revenue, margin or returns?
4. Which breakdown do you need: category, subcategory, brand, product, SKU or variant?
5. How do you treat promotions and bundles in your analysis?
6. Should you see only your own categories? Do categories overlap between managers?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Describe the last time you delisted or repriced a product. What data did you use?
2. How do you treat a bundle in your margin calculation?
3. What would you do if you saw return rates by brand?

## S4. Logistics & Warehouse Manager

**Core questions**

1. How many warehouses are there, and which stock exports exist (format, frequency)?
2. How do you detect stockouts and overstock today?
3. What counts as slow-moving stock (for example no sales for 90 days)?
4. Which carriers do you use, and what status data do they provide?
5. What does "on time" mean: promised date, carrier SLA?
6. How are orders shipped in several parcels recorded?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Describe the last stockout of a popular product. How did you find out?
2. How do you handle an order that ships in two parcels?
3. What list would you want printed every morning?

## S5. Online Marketing Specialist

**Core questions**

1. Which campaign cost data exists, in which tool and at what level of detail?
2. How do you define a new customer and CAC today?
3. Do you define ROAS on revenue or on margin?
4. How do you measure the effect of promotions?
5. Do you track newsletter results and consents?
6. Which reports would you open every week?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Pick your last campaign. How did you decide it was worth it?
2. How do you separate new from returning customers?
3. How would margin-based ROAS change your decisions?

## S6. Customer Service & Complaints Manager

**Core questions**

1. How are returns recorded: withdrawal versus complaint?
2. Which reason categories exist?
3. How are partial returns and refunds recorded?
4. Which report would reduce your workload most?
5. Which product, brand or supplier insights would help procurement?
6. Which free-text data (reviews, complaint descriptions) exists, and who may read it?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Give an example of a complaint pattern you noticed. How did you notice it?
2. How do you record a partial return today?
3. Who needs your insights most, and in what form?

## S7. Financial Controller

**Core questions**

1. How do you reconcile figures between the shop, marketplace panels and the ERP today? Where do they differ?
2. How do you define net sales (discounts, VAT, delivery, cancellations)?
3. How is cost of goods valued today, and how often does purchase cost change?
4. How are partial returns, cancelled orders and bundles treated in revenue?
5. What reconciliation accuracy do you expect?
6. At which level does the sales plan exist (month, channel, category)?

**Follow-up probes**

1. Walk me through the last reconciliation: where exactly did the difference come from?
2. When purchase cost changes, which cost applies to goods sold earlier?
3. How are cancelled orders and partial returns shown in revenue?

## Closing questions (all roles)

1. If we could deliver only three things in the next 12 weeks, which would you choose?
2. What would make you stop using a dashboard we build?
3. Which numbers do you trust least today, and why?
4. How often and in what form do you want to receive reports?
5. Who else in the company should we talk to, and what would they tell us?
